# Case Study + Customer Stories

> "Trusted by Acme, Microsoft, GitHub" is decoration. A specific
> story — "Acme reduced incident response by 70% in 8 weeks" — is
> conversion. Build that page well, every page on the site links to it.

## The proven structure (10 sections)

```
1. HERO       Customer name + headline result + photo + logo
2. CHALLENGE  What problem they faced (in their voice)
3. WHY US     Why they picked you over alternatives
4. SOLUTION   How they deployed / what they configured
5. RESULTS    The METRICS — three big numbers + supporting
6. QUOTE      Pull quote with attribution + photo
7. TIMELINE   Days / weeks / phases of rollout
8. TEAM       Who used it (titles, photos)
9. NEXT       What they're doing next with your product
10. CTA       Try it / Talk to sales
```

Length: 800-1500 words + 5-8 images. Reads in 3-5 minutes.

## Hero — the 10-second hook

```
ACME CORPORATION

How Acme retired 80% of their PagerDuty rules in 6 weeks
— and gave their on-call team weekends back

[Photo of named human or team]    [Acme logo]
[28%↓ alerts] [70%↓ MTTR] [4.1→4.8 NPS]
```

The 3-stat strip below the hero is the punch. Numbers are the most-
shared content on social.

## Challenge — show that you understand the pain

DON'T summarize: let the customer SPEAK.

```
"Every Tuesday at 2am we'd get paged. The same pattern, three weeks in
a row. By the time we found the rule that fired, our SLA was blown and
half the team was awake doing nothing useful."

— Jamie Chen, VP Engineering, Acme
```

Use a real quote from the customer interview. ALL READ-WORLD CONTEXT, no
generic problem statements.

## Why us — competitive differentiation in their voice

```
"We looked at Datadog and PagerDuty's native correlation. Both wanted us
to write rules to match our environment — but our environment changes
weekly. <Product> was the only one that learned from our incident
history. We were live in two hours."
```

This section converts technical buyers. Don't paraphrase — use the
customer's actual words about WHY YOU.

## Solution — concrete deployment

What did they DO with your product? Be specific:

```
Acme's deployment in 3 phases:

Phase 1 (Week 1-2): Connected 27 existing PagerDuty services via the
1-click integration. Imported 12 months of incident history.

Phase 2 (Week 3-4): Reviewed AI-generated rule recommendations. Approved
80% (the system caught patterns the team had been hand-tuning for years).

Phase 3 (Week 5-6): Trained the SRE team on the new triage flow. Deprecated
the old rules incrementally with rollback safety.

Architecture: [diagram showing their stack with you in it]
```

A diagram of THEIR architecture with you in it is gold.

## Results — three big numbers, then supporting

The big three (in the hero strip), THEN the depth:

```
KEY METRICS:
  ▼ 80% reduction in PagerDuty rules (300 → 60)
  ▼ 70% MTTR improvement (28 min → 9 min)
  ▲ NPS jump from 4.1 to 4.8 / 5

SUPPORTING:
  - Time-to-page: 90 sec → 12 sec
  - Avg alerts per shift: 12 → 4
  - On-call satisfaction: 6/10 → 9/10
  - Customer SLA breaches: 3/mo → 0
```

Make it scannable. Use ▼ ▲ to show direction. Include the BEFORE.

If you don't have hard numbers: don't write a case study. Wait until you
do. Vague results destroy credibility.

## Quote section — the social proof moment

```
[Large photo of named human]

"We retired 80% of our PagerDuty rules within 6 weeks. My team sleeps
through the night. I sleep through the night."

JAMIE CHEN
VP Engineering, Acme Corporation
Previously at Stripe + Datadog
```

Quote should:
- Be SPECIFIC (with numbers when possible).
- Show emotion / outcome ("sleep through the night").
- Be attributed to a REAL person with real credentials.
- Include LinkedIn link to prove they exist (optional but powerful).

NEVER use anonymous quotes. NEVER make them up.

## Timeline — the rollout story

```
Week 0:   Discovery call
Week 1-2: Integration setup
Week 3-4: AI rule generation + review
Week 5-6: Rollout + training
Week 7+:  Steady state — 60% time recovered for proactive work
```

Visual: horizontal timeline OR vertical step indicator. Helps prospects
estimate THEIR ROLLOUT time.

## Team section — humanize

```
The team that drove the deployment:

[Photo] Jamie Chen, VP Engineering
[Photo] Sarah Davis, Staff SRE  
[Photo] Marcus Lee, IC SRE
```

Optional but adds weight. Real photos + real titles.

## Future — what next

```
"With incident response sorted, we're rolling out <product> to our
support team next quarter. Then customer-facing reliability dashboards
in Q2 2026."
```

Shows ongoing relationship. Implies your product grows WITH them.

## CTAs — multiple paths

Bottom of page:

```
[Try <product> free]           [Read more stories]
[Book a demo]                  [See pricing]
```

OR a single contextualized CTA:

```
Most of our customers see meaningful results in 4-8 weeks.
[Start your free trial — no credit card]
```

Don't end on a quote and walk away. Close.

## Visual design — what to include

| Element | Why |
|---|---|
| Customer logo (large) | Trust transfer |
| Photos of real people | Humanizes; LinkedIn-verifiable |
| Architecture diagram | Helps technical buyer self-locate |
| Screenshots of THEIR usage (anonymized) | Concrete evidence |
| Pull quotes (bigger type) | Scanability |
| 3-stat hero strip | Sharable |
| Timeline graphic | Estimable |
| "On-brand" — match THEIR brand colors in the hero | Subtle premium signal |

NOT:
- Stock photos of "happy people in offices".
- Generic "results" pie charts.
- Marketing-team posed photos of customer logo on a screen.

## Length + format

Sweet spot: 800-1500 words. Long enough to be substantive; short enough
to read in 5 min.

Format options:
- **Long article** (most common).
- **Video case study** (2-3 min talking head with metrics overlay).
- **Podcast episode** (less popular but defensible for thought-leader brands).
- **Co-marketed webinar** featuring the customer.

Mix formats; some buyers prefer video, others prefer text.

## Sourcing case studies

Tips for getting customers to participate:
- ASK during peak satisfaction (right after major win).
- OFFER reciprocity (PR / co-marketing, swag, conference speakership).
- KEEP friction low: ghostwrite the draft, give them edit-only access.
- RESPECT IP: legal review on EVERY metric before publishing.
- COMPENSATE if asked (some industries: $500-5000 stipend acceptable).

## SEO + distribution

URL: `/customers/acme` (not /case-studies/2026-acme-corporation-rule-reduction).

Title: "How Acme retired 80% of their PagerDuty rules — Customer Story".

Open Graph: hero image with quote + stat strip overlay (1200×630).

LinkedIn / Twitter: ALL three social cards optimized (different headline
emphasis per platform).

Email: 1 newsletter blast at publication + 1 reminder 6 weeks later.

Sales enablement: rep slack channel with TL;DR + key quotes + the URL.

## Anti-patterns

- **Generic case study** with anonymized customer.
- **Vague metrics** ("significantly faster").
- **Quotes that don't sound like a real person** ("X has revolutionized
  our workflow").
- **One screenshot for every case study.** Different customers, different
  artifacts.
- **No CTA at the end.** Visitor liked it; what now?
- **Long-form case study with no skim path.** Pull quotes + metric strips.
- **PDF download required to read.** Friction; SEO loss.
- **Case study published once, never updated.** Refresh with new metrics
  yearly.
- **Generic stock photos.** Loses authenticity.
